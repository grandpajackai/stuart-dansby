03 /From the boardroom to the ring

After decades of building businesses and developing people, Stuart took on a new challenge. He began training at 48, entered his first fight at 55, and became a champion at 62 while facing opponents decades younger and overcoming life-altering injuries.

The award-winning documentary Taking the Fight chronicles that journey. The same principles that helped him grow in business and develop others shaped his preparation, learning, and performance in the ring.

Today, he turns those pillars into practical behaviors your audience can apply in their own work and lives.

Give your audience a shared understanding of how to approach challenges, take ownership, and help each other perform. Stuart connects each pillar to the priorities and experiences of the people in the room.

Explore the principles →
01

The Difference Between Stress and Challenge Is Perspective

+

Stress is what happens to you. Challenge is what you decide to do about it. Same event, same information. The difference is the story you tell yourself. The skill is catching the moment you can make the switch.

02

Everything Good in Life Comes From Discipline

+

Everything you do, every day, whether anyone is watching or not, has your signature on it. Health, relationships, wealth and growth are built through the daily choices nobody sees. Discipline is what shows up after motivation leaves.

03

Your Real Competition Is You

+

Your real competition is not the person across from you. It is who you were yesterday. Self-improvement is the standard. Beat yesterday’s version of yourself every day, then multiply that growth over a year.

04

Servant Leadership and the Power of the Team

+

Good people will put more pressure on themselves than you ever can. Resource them. Coach them. Empower them. Give them ownership and the freedom to make mistakes. You win or you learn.

05

Championship Performance

+

No one person or team arrives as a champion. Successful people do what unsuccessful people don’t want to do, because everything we do in life has our signature on it. Championship Performance is planned and practiced. It is the outcome created when the other four pillars work together.

Can the experience be tailored to our organization?

Yes. Stuart works with leadership and event organizers before your event to understand your audience, priorities, and culture, then connects the story and five pillars to your needs.

What is the difference between the two experiences?

The keynote introduces the five pillars in a 45–60-minute session, with a documentary or trailer screening and pre-event conversation. The full experience adds a 90-minute performance breakout and one-hour follow-ups with key leaders at 30 and 60 days.

Does our audience need an athletic background?

No. The framework connects Stuart’s business experience and championship journey to challenges people encounter in their own work and lives.
